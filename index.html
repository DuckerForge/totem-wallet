Totem Wallet

A wallet for the Solana Seeker that reads every transaction before you sign it, and an AI agent that trades a capped budget inside a collar you set.

What you see is what you sign.

01 · The problem

A name, a button, and your money is gone

When an app asks for a signature you see its name and an Approve button. You do not see how much leaves, to whom, or whether you are handing someone the right to take your tokens whenever they like. This is blind signing, and it is the first cause of stolen crypto.

The Seed Vault signs whatever the wallet hands it. The hardware is not the weak part. The screen in front of it is.

And agents make it worse: to trade for you, an AI needs a key, and a key in an agent's hands is a key in a prompt injection's hands.

02 · The answer

A receipt before the fingerprint

Every request, from a dApp over Mobile Wallet Adapter, from Send, Swap, a Blink, the bridge, an NFC sticker or the agent, is simulated on chain first and shown as a plain receipt: what leaves, to whom, the fee, the risks, the address's trust badge.

Then hold to confirm. Then the fingerprint. Keys never leave the vault.

Here the attack is a drainer. The receipt names the real amount and the real recipient, and the signature is blocked before the Seed Vault is even asked.

03 · How it reads the truth

The effect, never the claim

  • On-chain simulation, legacy and v0 with lookup tables: real balance changes, including splits hidden across several wallets.
  • Risk engine: drains, unlimited approvals, authority changes, wallet takeover, a foreign fee payer, burn addresses, unexpected signers, bundles.
  • Look-alike detection against your own contacts, which is how address poisoning actually works.
  • Anti-TOCTOU: it re-simulates the instant before signing and refuses if the state drifted between what you read and what you sign.
  • Five attacks built in: a drainer, a look-alike, an unlimited approval, a state drift and a safe payment, all offline, so anyone can try the defences with no dApp.

04 · The agent

It gets a budget, never your seed

You open a budget: a second key, created on the phone, capped in SOL and in days, apart from your wallet. The agent hunts across Solana with the gates the winning bots use, buys a slice, sells at your target or your stop, and closes the budget when it expires.

You set the collar: how much a move, how much a day, how fast, and above what it must ask for your fingerprint. A policy engine reads the simulated effect of every move, never the agent's claim. Your own rules, written in plain Markdown, can only close doors, never open them.

The collar is the product. The agent can be wrong, hallucinate or be injected: it cannot spend more than the budget, and it cannot reach your wallet.

05 · The agent that lies

An AI hands over a transaction. We check it against the chain.

An agent running on a computer holds no key here. It gives the phone a transaction and a declared intent: what it says it is doing. Totem simulates the real bytes and compares the claim with the effect.

  • Every outflow must be the declared one, in the declared amount.
  • A swap must actually bring back the token it promised.
  • No hidden delegation, authority change or durable nonce, ever.

Honest agentLiar, blocked

If the agent declares a swap and the transaction is a transfer, the signature is refused and the screen says exactly what did not add up. It never judges whether a trade is smart. It verifies that the agent is telling the truth.

06 · ORE

The grid, read from the program itself

ORE is a game on Solana: a five by five grid, one round a minute. It is written in Steel, so there is no IDL to lean on. Totem decodes the program by hand and shows what a square really costs and what it can pay before anything is put on it.

  • The live round, the emptiest squares, and how many miners are on the grid right now.
  • A Deploy or a Claim from any dApp shows up in the receipt as what it is: a wager.
  • The agent can dig from its budget, under the same collar, and bring the gains home in ORE when the budget closes.

07 · The Seeker, and SKR

A number no balance shows

The Genesis Token is one per device, so the crowd is real people holding a Seeker: 120,520 wallets, enumerated once on chain, about ten and a half thousand of them active enough to watch continuously.

Claim and stake moves SKR inside the Guardians' program, so a list built from token accounts finds nothing there. Totem decodes that program and shows the position, with the yield measured from the share price, not derived from tokenomics.

08 · Why it sticks

It stays with you when the app is closed

  • A bubble over any app and a widget on the home screen, both showing what the agent is doing and how the wallet is.
  • Pay by touch: the phone emulates a payment tag, so someone taps and pays.
  • A sticker: write a Solana Pay request onto a blank NFC tag costing cents.
  • Contacts by touch, each card signed by the phone's attestation key, which is what closes the door on address poisoning.
  • A proof of payment as a QR this phone signed, verified by another phone with no explorer and no network.
